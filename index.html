Eight reasons teams switch.

The small things that compound. Each one came from a specific customer conversation — none are theoretical.

One image, every device

The same signed ISO boots on a 2018 ThinPad, a 2025 Mac mini, and a fleet of Raspberry Pi 5s. No driver hunt, no respins.

Updates that don't bite

Atomic, signed, and reversible. A failed boot rolls back automatically to the last known good state, before you even hear about it.

Your keys, your image

Sovereign edition hands you the signing key. The build runs on your hardware, the SBOM lives on your network, the audit trail is yours.

Console that scales

Push a profile to ten thousand machines with one command. MDM-style enrollment, policy inheritance, and ring-based rollouts.

Built for audits

SLSA L3 provenance, in-toto attestations, and a published SBOM for every release. Auditors stop asking after the first call.

Works air-gapped

Install, update, and recover without ever touching the public internet. The private mirror is one package, two ports, three commands.

Telemetry, opt-in

There is no off-switch because there is no on-switch. What you share is up to you, and it leaves your network over a documented channel.

Designed in the open

RFCs, roadmap, and changelog are public. Every release ships with a written post-mortem of what didn't make it and why.

Support that codes

The engineer who answers your ticket is the engineer who wrote the code. No tier-one, no escalation queues, no scripts.

Three steps, under five minutes.

No accounts, no driver hunt. Flash, boot, and you're working in a session that's reproducible across every machine you own.

1

Download

Pull the image from a public mirror. Verify the SHA-256 against the checksum we publish with every release.

$ curl -LO aarambh.os/3.0.iso
2

Flash & boot

Write to a USB 3 stick with dd, balena, or gnome-disk. Boot from it. The installer runs in under ninety seconds.

$ dd if=aarambh.iso of=/dev/sdX bs=8M
3

Sign in & go

Bring your own identity — SSO, hardware token, or local. Updates stage in the background; you keep working.

$ aarambh-update --apply

Things people actually ask.

Yes. The Personal edition is free forever — full desktop, public updates, no telemetry, no account. We charge for teams (MDM, private mirror, SLA) and for sovereign deployments (air-gap, custom signing key).

If it's a 64-bit x86 or ARM machine from 2018 or later, almost certainly yes. We test against a reference set of forty devices and publish the full compatibility list with every release.

Every image is signed by the Gentix Science build key. The UKI verifies the signature at boot, and a failed signature rolls the system back to the last known good state automatically. The signing key fingerprint is published on the website and rotated on a documented schedule.

Yes — the entire install can run air-gapped, and the Sovereign edition ships a private update mirror that lives on your network. Telemetry is opt-in even on the free edition; there is no off-switch because there is no on-switch.

Flatpak and DPKG5 are both supported out of the box. Most modern Linux desktop apps install without modification. For anything that needs a specific runtime, the console can ship a per-app container image to the fleet.

A 1,000-machine fleet moves from staged to live in under ninety minutes, on a 100 Mbps mirror. Most of that time is the machines rebooting — Aarambh never blocks the user; updates apply on the next natural restart.
